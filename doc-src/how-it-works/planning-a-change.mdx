Consider turning `aa b cc` into `xx b zz`. Two reasonable plans:

```
two edits   aa→xx, move over b, cc→zz    cwxx<Esc> ww cwzz<Esc>   12 keystrokes, 4 chars changed
one edit    delete everything, retype     Cxx b zz<Esc>             9 keystrokes, 7 chars changed
```

Pure edit distance always prefers the first: it changes fewer characters.
Moving between regions and re-entering insert mode cost keystrokes too, which
is what makes the second plan cheaper here. The planner's job is to weigh those
against each other.

## What a plan is

A plan works left to right through the change. At each point it either
**deletes** a run of the initial text, **types** a run of the goal text, or
**moves** across a run both sides share. Throughout, `i` counts the characters
of the initial text consumed and `j` the characters of the goal produced; a
finished plan has consumed and produced everything.

A maximal run of deleting and typing between two moves is one **planned edit**
(a *region*). The widget below shows the regions the planner picks and what each
one costs.

<PlanWidget />

Try `the quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog` → `the quick red fox leaps over the lazy dog`:
the planner keeps ` fox ` and makes two edits. Then shorten the gap between the
edits until it decides one edit through the middle is cheaper.

## What a plan costs

```
sum(delete costs) + sum(insert costs) + sum(move costs)
```

The shared prefix and suffix are free. Every optimal plan starts at the first
differing character and ends at the last, so reaching them from the cursor costs
the same for every plan and cannot change the ranking.

### Pricing one step

A step advances `(i, j)` in one of three ways:

| step   | commands                          | cost                                         | `(i, j) →`   |
|--------|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|--------------|
| move   | `l` `w` `W` `j` `}` `$`           | counted-motion tiling                        | `(i+x, j+x)` |
| delete | `x` `dw` `dW` `dd` `dap` `D` `d0` | counted-operator tiling                      | `(i+x, j)`   |
| type   | typed text                        | insert-mode entry + per-key effort + `<Esc>` | `(i, j+y)`   |

A delete followed directly by typing is the `c` form (`cw`, `cc`, `s`), which
saves the entry key: in the widget, a region that both deletes and types pays
`<Esc>` but not `i`. Counted commands cost their digit keystrokes plus a
concave cognitive penalty (roughly `sqrt(k)`), shared with the downstream
search.

Both oracles are deliberately coarse keystroke counts. The planner only ranks
partitions; the [search](/Vimficiency/docs/how-it-works/searching/) re-prices the winning sequence exactly,
with the per-key effort model of your keyboard. The planner has no sequence of
its own, which is why the widget shows costs and not commands.
